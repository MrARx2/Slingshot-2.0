# Hovercraft V3 — Physical Handling, Surface Following, UI, and Recorder Handoff

Date: 2026-08-01  
Project: Slingshot 2.0

Active hover profile: `hover.apex.systems.01` version 2

## 1. Purpose and current state

This handoff records the architecture after the first data-driven Track Test correction pass. The pass addressed the recorded loss of ride height in the loop, separation from descending and banked track sections, unused physical hover-thruster capacity, and excessive runtime UI coverage.

The governing rule remains unchanged:

> Player and software intent may only move the craft through installed physical parts. Handling controllers do not apply hidden Rigidbody forces or torques, do not make the body kinematic, and do not teleport it during normal driving.

The new surface-following logic does not implement track adhesion. It measures the physical surface, calculates a requested acceleration, and asks the installed bottom or roof thrusters to produce the corresponding force. Those requests still pass through actuator allocation, firmware availability, power allocation, thermal limits, spool response, installed hardware force limits, and `Rigidbody.AddForceAtPosition` at each thruster's real force origin.

## 2. Authoritative runtime flow

The normal driving path is:

1. `V3PilotInputAdapter` reads player input and publishes pilot intent.
2. `V3CraftMainframe` owns the intent bus, software scheduler, observations, system state, and control routing.
3. `V3ControllerPipeline` begins an actuator frame and calls the physical-system controllers.
4. Controllers calculate requests only:
   - `V3DriveController` requests propulsion/braking thrusters.
   - `V3HoverController` requests bottom hover and roof control thrusters.
   - `V3VectorController` requests strafe/yaw thrusters.
   - `V3TractionController` requests installed actuators for grip intent.
   - `V3GimbalController` requests the physical gimbal actuator.
5. `V3ActuatorCommandRouter` combines channels and priorities for each installed thruster.
6. Thruster firmware, `V3PowerDistributor`, and thermal protection limit the request.
7. `RuntimeThrusterInstance.ApplyPowerGrant` applies the actual force with `AddForceAtPosition` at the part's force origin and current physical orientation.
8. PhysX integrates the non-kinematic craft Rigidbody, collisions, gravity, and aerodynamic forces.

No handling controller calls `AddForce`, `AddTorque`, `MovePosition`, or `MoveRotation`.

## 3. Physical force authority audit

The runtime force-application sites are intentionally limited:

- `RuntimeThrusterInstance`: physical installed thruster force at the part origin.
- `RuntimeAerodynamicFinInstance`: aerodynamic force generated by each installed fin at its center of pressure.
- `V3CraftAerodynamicsRuntime`: physical chassis drag/downforce at the serialized chassis center of pressure.
- `V3WorldEnvironmentProvider`: authoritative sampled world gravity, applied as an acceleration field. It disables Unity's duplicate built-in gravity only while a valid world sample exists.
- PhysX collision/contact forces: generated by colliders and the physics solver.

The velocity/pose writes found outside those sites are explicit recovery or regression-test operations:

- `V3FreeDriveSession.ResetCraft`: manual/kill-plane reset only; the recorder marks the discontinuity and starts a new attempt.
- `V3ParityRunController`: development regression harness only.

There is no runtime handling assignment to `Rigidbody.isKinematic`.

## 4. Why the loop previously failed

The systems craft's bottom and roof definitions provide:

- Nominal force per thruster: 160,000 N.
- Normal output multiplier: 7.
- Maximum normal physical force per thruster: 1,120,000 N.
- Four bottom thrusters: up to 4,480,000 N away from the sensed surface.
- Four roof thrusters: up to 4,480,000 N toward the sensed surface.

The controller previously clamped every automatic hover command to the range 0–1. That restricted each bank to its 160,000 N nominal level even though the installed parts, firmware, power model, and thermal model supported a normal output multiplier of 7. The recorded loop required roughly 2.0–3.0 MN of inward force at the observed/target speed, while the controller could not deliberately request the installed headroom.

The controller now clamps each request to that installed thruster's serialized `NormalOutputMultiplier`, not to an abstract 1.0 ceiling. Emergency overload remains separate and is not used by automatic surface following.

## 5. Surface measurement

The bottom hover assemblies are also the physical reference points for hover sensing. Each physics submission:

- sphere-casts from the installed bottom thruster force origin;
- casts opposite the thruster's current physical thrust direction;
- uses the serialized track layer mask and sensing range;
- records hit distance, normal, origin, direction, and fallback use in `V3HoverProbeSnapshot`;
- averages valid probe distances, separation velocities, and normals;
- requires at least two valid probes before deriving curvature feed-forward.

The fallback probe only finds a real collider and projects missed corner probes onto that detected surface plane. It does not query `TrackGenerator` geometry, spline data, section metadata, or an invisible racing line. Procedural control therefore remains based on physical observations rather than privileged track truth.

## 6. Signed curvature following

Consecutive physical surface normals determine whether the track curves away from or toward the craft.

The signed feed-forward acceleration is conceptually:

```text
normalRate = (currentNormal - previousNormal) / fixedDeltaTime
tangentVelocity = velocity projected onto the detected surface
curveAcceleration = -speed * dot(normalRate, tangentDirection) * feedForward
```

The result is filtered and clamped to the serialized acceleration limit.

- Positive curvature acceleration means the path bends away from the material toward the detected surface normal. Bottom hover thrusters provide the physical support. This is the main loop/corkscrew case.
- Negative curvature acceleration means a convex crest bends toward the material. Roof thrusters provide physical force toward the surface.
- A flat surface produces zero curvature feed-forward.
- Invalid/no surface evidence clears the curvature state rather than continuing an invisible force.

Gravity compensation uses only the component of the sampled physical gravity opposite the detected surface normal. This avoids applying a universal world-up weight correction on walls or at the top of a loop.

## 7. Bottom hover-thruster request

For a valid surface, the bottom bank combines:

- the required positive signed curvature acceleration;
- the appropriate surface-normal gravity compensation;
- target-height position error;
- surface-normal separation velocity damping;
- manual lift intent;
- physical roll/pitch stabilization requests.

Acceleration becomes installed-part output by dividing the required craft force (`mass * acceleration`) by the sum of the installed bottom thrusters' nominal force. Each part then enforces its own normal-output limit.

Surface-alignment angular acceleration is still converted through the actual Rigidbody inertia tensor to a requested world torque. `V3ActuatorCommandRouter.SubmitWrench` allocates that torque across the eight bottom/roof thrusters. The controller never calls `Rigidbody.AddTorque`.

## 8. Roof-thruster surface capture

Automatic roof capture is active only when:

- physical surface evidence exists;
- stabilization is enabled;
- the mainframe/control pipeline permits the hover/stabilization command.

The roof request combines:

```text
distanceError = max(0, sensedDistance - targetHeight - deadband)
captureAcceleration = distanceError * captureStrength
                    + separationVelocity * captureDamping
                    + max(0, -signedCurvatureAcceleration)
```

The result is clamped to the serialized roof-capture acceleration cap, converted from force to installed-part output, and submitted to the four `Control.*.Top` thrusters on the stabilization channel.

This means:

- a craft moving away from a descending surface begins firing real roof thrusters;
- a convex crest can request roof force before a large height error develops;
- a craft already moving toward the target reduces the request through damping;
- loss of all physical surface evidence removes automatic capture authority;
- power starvation, firmware safe state, thermal lockout, spool rate, damage/disable state, and hardware limits still reduce or remove the actual force.

Manual downforce and pitch remain separate pilot requests on the manual actuator channel.

## 10. Reset behavior

`V3ControllerPipeline.ResetDynamicState` now also clears `V3HoverController` surface-normal history, filtered curvature acceleration, average distance/separation state, and roof-capture state. A reset therefore cannot carry a pre-reset curvature request into a new attempt.

The recorder already receives `V3CraftResetNotification`, increments the attempt index, marks the next sample as an external-state discontinuity, and emits a reset event. Reset motion is not treated as spontaneous craft acceleration.

## 11. UI architecture after cleanup

### Startup state

Only the pilot HUD is visible when a development scene starts. The following begin closed:

- Craft Systems console.
- Full controls legend.
- Session Recorder overlay.
- Thruster debug visualization.
- World debug display.
- Legacy thermal and cockpit-warning overlays.

The Track Test, Handling Track, and CraftLab scene assets explicitly serialize the systems console and controls legend as closed so old scene overrides cannot defeat the new defaults.

### Pilot HUD

`V3FreeDriveHud` now renders a classic racing-style horizontal strip at the bottom:

- large numeric speed centered at the bottom;
- no dark telemetry box or border;
- altitude and power mode to the left;
- stabilization and maximum temperature to the right;
- a small craft name above the speed;
- compact reticle and transient status/warning text.

### Always-visible key hints

- Top middle: `F7 SESSION RECORDER`.
- Top right: `F4 CRAFT SYSTEMS`.
- Top right below it: `F2 DEBUG`.
- Top left: `F3 CONTROLS    F1 HUD`.

### Controls

- `F1`: show/hide pilot HUD and hints.
- `F2`: show/hide physical thruster direction/firing/temperature debug.
- `F3`: show/hide the compact controls legend.
- `F4`: show/hide the Craft Systems console.
- `O` / `P`: previous/next systems page while the console is open.
- `I`: minimize/restore the open systems console.

The F4 defect was caused by the console having no F4 visibility handler. It now toggles `ShowConsole` directly. The unused legacy `V3SystemsDebugDisplay` is not installed on assembled crafts; the scene-owned `V3SystemsConsoleController` remains the single systems UI.

## 12. Recorder overlay behavior

`V3DiagnosticRecorderOverlay` no longer renders an idle panel.

- Before F7: no recorder panel, only the small top hint.
- F7 starts recording and opens a compact top-center status strip.
- While recording: time, sample/event/drop counts, track position, section, world-contact state, and craft belief are visible.
- F7 stops recording; the strip remains while automatic export is active, then disappears.
- F8 adds a manual marker.
- F9 stops if necessary and exports; the strip is visible during export.

## 13. Diagnostics and telemetry changes

Diagnostic schema version is now 5.

Each craft-belief sample includes:

- believed surface distance;
- believed surface separation velocity;
- signed surface-tracking acceleration;
- roof-capture acceleration;
- roof-capture normalized output;
- existing target height, surface normal, alignment request, range, and grounded state.

The ride-height chart CSV now includes true distance, believed distance, target height, separation velocity, signed curvature acceleration, roof-capture acceleration, and roof output. This allows the next report to prove whether the system sensed the descent, requested the appropriate physical bank, and received the expected ride-height result.

The diagnostic craft snapshot and Markdown report include the complete serialized curvature and roof-capture configuration.

The Craft Systems Hover page also displays live:

- average sensed distance and separation velocity;
- signed curvature request (`+hover / -roof`);
- roof-capture acceleration and requested output.

## 17. Required next driving validation

The implementation is ready for a new Track Test recording, but the numerical tuning is not considered final until that drive is analyzed.

Use this sequence:

1. Let Unity finish importing/compiling and verify the Console has no errors.
2. Open `Assets/HovercraftV3/Scenes/Development/V3_TrackTest.unity`.
3. Enter Play Mode. Confirm only the bottom pilot HUD and top key hints appear.
4. Press F4 twice and verify the systems console opens and closes.
5. Press F2 and watch the bottom and roof arrows through the loop, banked curve, and descending section.
6. Press F7 at the starting line. Drive at the intended test pace without changing asset values during the run.
7. Press F7 to stop and allow export to complete.
8. Compare the new report and `charts/ride_height.csv` against the previous run.

The key acceptance signals are:

- loop ride height no longer collapses to approximately 0.3 m;
- positive signed curvature acceleration rises through the loop and bottom-thruster output uses values above 1 where required;
- descending/convex transitions produce negative signed curvature and/or positive roof-capture output;
- roof debug arrows visibly fire toward the physical surface;
- the craft remains inside the 80 m physical sensing envelope through ordinary track transitions;
- device power, firmware, and thermal telemetry explain any denied force;
- no unexplained force/torque residual or reset discontinuity appears.

If the craft still leaves the track after the bottom/roof banks saturate, tune installed force capacity or track geometry based on the recorded saturation. If requests are late but not saturated, tune sensing range, curvature response, capture strength, or damping. Do not add a direct Rigidbody adhesion force.

## 18. Rules for future agents

1. Never add stabilizing `Rigidbody.AddForce` or `AddTorque` calls to a controller.
2. Never use `isKinematic`, `MovePosition`, `MoveRotation`, or pose snapping for normal driving.
3. Keep all control motion attributable to installed devices and real force origins.
4. Treat track spline/section data as forensic truth only, not as craft control input.
5. Preserve firmware, power, thermal, damage/enable, and scheduler failure paths.
6. Store authoritative tuning in serialized build/system/part definitions, not runtime-only controller fields.
7. Regeneration utilities must reproduce the same serialized values as checked-in assets.
8. Record both requested and actual device behavior before changing capacity.
9. Separate reset/test teleport utilities from runtime driving authority and mark every discontinuity in diagnostics.
10. Use the next forensic report to tune one physical domain at a time.
